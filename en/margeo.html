A costing spreadsheet goes wrong without anything flagging it

A cost price worked out by hand always produces a number. The trouble is that it stays plausible even when it is wrong — and a selling price gets set on it for months. Three mistakes turn up in almost every spreadsheet we open:

  • Losses are added instead of divided. A 50% trim loss doubles the cost of the product; it does not raise it by half. The correct formula is quantity × price ÷ (1 − loss); × (1 + loss) understates it every time, and the heavier the trimming, the worse it gets.
  • Food cost is taken from purchases rather than from consumption — opening stock + purchases − closing stock. A month spent filling the cellar then looks catastrophic, and the next one excellent, without a single dish having changed.
  • Supplier prices go stale. A recipe card built in January and never reopened describes a menu that no longer exists as soon as two or three prices have moved.

What Margeo works out for you

  • Supplier invoices read by AI — photograph the invoice: the product, the quantity and the price per kilo are read and matched to your catalogue. No re-keying, and the prices on your recipe cards follow by themselves.
  • True cost price per dish, with trim losses divided rather than added, and cost taken from what was actually consumed.
  • Margin per dish and per menu category, with price spread by the Omnès method — a French menu-pricing approach, not to be confused with Anglo-Saxon menu engineering, which ranks dishes on margin × popularity and answers a different question.
  • Monthly profit and loss account kept up to date without re-keying, with till sales imported as CSV.
  • Thirteen-week cash forecast — the horizon that lets you see a gap coming before it arrives.
  • Stocktaking and cellar: stock is a sum of movements — last count plus everything since — not a counter, which would lose its history the day it goes wrong.

Who it is for

Independent restaurants, bars, brasseries and caterers who track their margins in a spreadsheet, or who have stopped tracking them at all. Margeo suits a single site as well as a small group: each site has its own data, its own suppliers and its own profit and loss account.

What it replaces

The folder of recipe cards, the food-cost spreadsheet, manual invoice entry, and the monthly meeting with the accountant where you discover the result six weeks after the event. The eight Margeo calculators give a taste of the same maths, and open from the demo.

Frequently asked questions

Do invoices have to be re-keyed?

No. They are photographed and read. That is what makes the method survive: margin tracking that costs an hour of data entry a week gets abandoned within a month.

Does Margeo replace my till system?

No, it complements it. Sales are imported from your till as CSV; Margeo handles what a till does not calculate — the cost of what was sold.

What about my accountant?

They stay your accountant. Margeo gives you the profit and loss account mid-month, while a decision is still possible; the statutory accounts come afterwards.

Is my data isolated from other restaurants?

Yes, by design: every query runs in the context of your own site, and a query with no context returns no rows rather than all of them.
