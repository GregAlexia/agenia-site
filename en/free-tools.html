Eight calculators for restaurants

Included in Margeo, published by AgenIA

Cost price, recommended selling price, break-even point, the gap between a delivery note and its invoice, the allergen table… Eight calculators that run in the browser, included in Margeo and open from the demo.

Open the calculators

The eight calculators

  • Recipe cost price — the true food cost of a dish, trim losses included. A 50% loss doubles the cost of an ingredient: it is divided, quantity × price ÷ (1 − loss), not added.
  • Selling price of a dish — from food cost to menu price, by target food-cost percentage, by multiplier or by cash margin, with the applicable VAT rate.
  • Multiplier — the step from food cost to price, and what the multiplier hides when the dish is cheap.
  • Prime cost — food cost plus fully loaded payroll. This is the ratio that predicts whether a business survives: above 70%, no other line of the accounts rescues profitability.
  • Break-even point — the turnover at which the month is covered.
  • Delivery discrepancy — what was delivered set against what was invoiced, line by line, with the complaint ready to send.
  • Allergen table — the declaration of the fourteen allergens you have to display (EU Regulation 1169/2011).
  • Wine list pricing — whether a wine list holds together, bottle by bottle.

Your recipes never leave your browser

The maths runs on your machine: none of your recipe data is sent anywhere or kept. The calculators are included in Margeo — the demo is enough to try them, with an email address and no credit card. What they do not do is keep your figures up to date all year — that is the job of the margin software, which reads the invoices and recalculates without re-keying.

Frequently asked questions

How do you work out the cost price of a dish?

By adding up the cost of each ingredient at the quantity actually used — so after trimming. That is where the trap is: the loss is divided. A fillet bought at €20/kg with 50% trim comes out at €40 per usable kilo, not €30. The formula is quantity × price ÷ (1 − loss).

What counts as a good prime cost?

Below 65%, there is enough left to pay the rent, the energy and the insurance and still show a result. Between 65 and 70%, the balance holds with no room for error. Above 70%, the problem is no longer in fixed costs: it is in the menu or in the rota.

Should we think in percentages or in cash margin?

In cash. A dish at 25% food cost that brings in €6 matters less than a dish at 35% that brings in €14. It is the euros that pay the rent, not the percentages.

Do I need an account to use them?

Access to Margeo, yes. The quickest way in is the demo: an email address opens it, with no credit card, and the calculators are there exactly as they are in an account. They were open to anyone until 22 September 2026; they are now a feature of the software.

Are my recipes saved anywhere?

No. The calculators run in the browser: nothing you type leaves your device.
